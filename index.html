01 / The challenge

A clearer target.
A more adaptable prediction.

Underwater tracking has two connected challenges: unreliable visual depth and target motion that changes.

AFMPC connects stereo perception with adaptive predictive control in a position-based visual-servoing system. A target-depth mask combines color, disparity and temporal consistency. Separate filters stabilize the image center and depth before reconstructing the target’s relative state.

Yaw regulation is separated from translation. The controller blends stationary-target and moving-target predictions using their historical errors, then computes bounded translational commands. Video replay, simulation and pool trials evaluate the complete system.

02 / The approach

From pixels to predictive control.

One integrated loop: select reliable observations, reconstruct the relative state, regulate heading and adapt the prediction.

  1. 01

    Select reliable target depth

    Color and disparity cues isolate target pixels within a detection box. Temporal recovery fills gaps when the current-frame overlap becomes sparse.

    Color + disparity + time
  2. 02

    Reconstruct a stable state

    A Kalman filter estimates depth and depth rate; an α–β filter tracks the image center. Their outputs form the target’s 3D relative position and velocity.

    Separate estimation channels
  3. 03

    Decouple heading and translation

    A HOLD/TURN state machine and cascaded PID keep the vehicle oriented toward the target. A predicted yaw sequence keeps translational optimization a convex quadratic program.

    Decoupled heading regulation
  4. 04

    Fuse motion hypotheses

    Stationary and constant-velocity models compete through historical multi-step prediction errors. Adaptive weights combine them inside the MPC horizon.

    Adaptive model-fusion MPC
Perception

Depth should come from the target.

Background pixels and stereo mismatches can distort bounding-box depth. The target-depth mask selects a more consistent set of pixels, with temporal recovery when needed.

Prediction

A moving target can stop.
The model should respond.

Rather than committing to one motion hypothesis, AFMPC evaluates past predictions and updates the balance between stationary and constant-velocity models.

Its translational controller uses the fused prediction with force and force-rate limits. The hardware trials include a deliberate move-to-stop transition.

Heading regulation matters.

Evaluation scope

Validated in video replay, Unity simulation and a controlled 4 m × 2 m × 1 m pool. Disparity refinement, sustained observation loss and open-water generalization remain directions for future work.
